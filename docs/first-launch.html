First launch

A few quick steps get your local PHP environment running. Only one of them asks for your password.

Phun opens with a short intro: the P gathers from the dots, the name settles in, and it fades once Phun is ready. If your system asks for reduced motion, it holds still.

The welcome screen comes next. It stays until you finish it: quitting or restarting part-way brings you back to the step you need.

  1. Daemon

    Phun installs its daemon, the small background service that serves your .test applications, answers DNS and runs databases. It never runs as root; the app only talks to it.

    If ports 80 and 443 are already taken by another program, the step offers free ports to use instead, and the same for the DNS port. You can skip this and change them later in Settings.

  2. PHP

    The latest PHP version is selected for you and becomes your default. The download can take a few minutes; you can add more versions later.

    This step can also add phun to your PATH, so phun, php and the tools Phun installs run directly in your terminal. It is optional.

  3. Trust

    For a green padlock on .test, Phun trusts its local certificate authority, which signs your HTTPS certificates, and installs the DNS resolver that points .test names at Phun. This asks for your password once; nothing after it does.

    If it cannot finish, retry there or run this in a terminal:

    phun elevate
  4. Done

    Phun is ready. Applications, PHP and services are managed from the Overview.

Without admin rights

No admin rights to hand? Every application is also reachable at http://localhost/~name.test, with no resolver or certificate needed.

Finding your way

  • The Overview lists your applications, with Services, Diagnostics and Settings beside them. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4 open those four in order (Cmd on macOS), and Ctrl+5 opens Mail.
  • The command palette, Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on macOS), reaches every page, application, Artisan command and action. Matching is forgiving: "tnkr" finds Tinker.
  • Themes. Daybreak and Nightfall, or follow the system, under Settings › Appearance. The choice reaches every window and the editor.
  • Key binds. Settings › Key binds gives any action in the command palette a key of your own.
  • The tray. Closing the window keeps Phun running in the tray, with the daemon's state and Start, Restart and Stop.

When something is off

Diagnostics runs Phun's health checks for DNS, certificates, ports and PHP, each with a one-click fix. From the terminal:

phun doctor  # check the setup
phun doctor fix  # repair what it safely can

Diagnostics › Report gathers what a problem report needs in one file: versions, services, Phun's processes, a .test lookup, health checks and recent logs. Passwords, tokens, keys, email addresses and your user name are removed before you see it.
